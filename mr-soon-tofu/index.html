On spice

Every soon tofu is made to a spice level you choose, from none at all to the hottest the kitchen makes. If it is your first time, mild is a good place to start. You can always ask for a side of the chili oil.

The stone bowl

What to expect when the pot lands on your table.

Soondubu is soft tofu stew. Ours is made in the kitchen, not from a base, and it comes to the table in a stone pot still boiling hard enough that it keeps cooking in front of you. A raw egg comes on the side. Slide it in, stir it through, and let the broth set it.

Around the pot the table fills up: rice, and roughly fifteen small dishes that change with what the kitchen has that day. They are part of the meal, not an extra, and they come back when they run low. Plan to be here a little while. This is a sit-down, slow-down kind of dinner.

Photo: the stone pot, mid-boil

Three things to know

The pot keeps cooking

It arrives boiling. Give it a minute before the first spoonful so it does not catch you out.

The banchan are unlimited

Ask the server for more of any of them. There is no charge and no limit.

The egg is yours to add

Some people stir it straight in, some poach it whole in the broth. Both are right.
